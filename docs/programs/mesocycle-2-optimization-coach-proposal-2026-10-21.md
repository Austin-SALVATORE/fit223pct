# Fit223PCT — Coach Proposal
## Mesocycle 2: Home Full Body Optimization

**Status:** Coach-approved proposal for Agent Team review  
**Baseline:** Mesocycle 2 — Build, rendered from program seed `f8b20ccd`, 21 October 2026  
**Objective:** Fat loss with balanced hypertrophy and continued strength progression  
**Training frequency:** Three Full Body sessions per week

---

## 1. Background and rationale

The athlete has been following a three-day Full Body A/B/C rotation, with four working sets for almost every movement.

The current weekly rotation contains:

| Session | Movements | Working sets |
|---|---:|---:|
| Full Body A | 6 | 24 |
| Full Body B | 6 | 24 |
| Full Body C | 7 | 28 |
| **Total** | **19** | **76** |

Following a recent Full Body C session, the athlete reported substantial fatigue and felt that seven movements, each with four working sets, had become excessive.

The athlete has also acquired a **Decathlon Domyos freestanding adjustable parallel-bar station**, which creates opportunities to introduce calisthenics-based pushing and pulling movements.

Additionally, the athlete wishes to remove Barbell Hip Thrust because its setup and execution are inconvenient and potentially unsafe in the current home environment.

### Coaching assessment

The issue is not that Full Body training has become inherently unsuitable.

The more likely programming problem is excessive session workload relative to the athlete's current recovery budget, particularly while maintaining a caloric deficit.

The previous blanket increase to four working sets for every movement was too broad.

The revised program should:

- Preserve three weekly Full Body sessions.
- Maintain meaningful squat, hinge, unilateral leg, push and pull training.
- Reduce redundant accessory volume.
- Introduce appropriate parallel-bar movements.
- Remove Barbell Hip Thrust.
- Preserve dedicated abdominal training in every session.
- Maintain measurable progression through Fit223's existing log-based carry-forward mechanism.

Do not interpret reduced set count as abandoning progressive overload.

## 2. Training split decision

**Retain three-day Full Body A/B/C.**

Do not switch to a four-day split at this stage.

The athlete has explicitly chosen three weekly resistance-training days, with balanced hypertrophy and fat loss as the primary objective.

The current rotation remains:

- Monday — Full Body A
- Wednesday — Full Body B
- Friday — Full Body C

Sessions advance by completed-session count, not by blindly following calendar weekdays.

If a session is missed, resume with the next uncompleted A/B/C session.

Keep the existing post-strength Zone 2 arrangement, subject to recovery.

## 3. Proposed Full Body A — Squat Emphasis

**Focus:** Quadriceps, upper chest, back, lateral deltoids and triceps.

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Decision |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Goblet Squat | 4 | Retain |
| 2 | Incline Dumbbell Press | 4 | Retain |
| 3 | Barbell Bent-Over Row | 4 | Retain |
| 4 | Parallel Bar Dip | 2 | New |
| 5 | Dumbbell Lateral Raise | 3 | Reduce volume |
| 6 | Plank | 2 | Reduce volume |

**Total: 6 movements, 19 working sets.**

### Changes

Remove Overhead Triceps Extension from Session A.

Introduce Parallel Bar Dip in its place.

The rationale is to prioritize a multi-joint movement that trains the chest and triceps while eliminating an overhead accessory movement that is not essential to the current program.

### Dip onboarding

The athlete has not yet established a baseline for unassisted parallel-bar dips.

Therefore:

- Initial target: approximately 2 × 5–8 controlled reps, only if achievable with good technique.
- Assisted repetitions are acceptable.
- Maintain approximately 2–3 technically achievable reps in reserve.
- Use a comfortable shoulder range of motion.
- Avoid aggressive bottom-position shoulder extension.
- Stop if shoulder, sternum or anterior chest pain occurs.

If this target cannot be performed safely, use a suitable assisted variation or retain an existing pressing alternative temporarily.

**Do not treat the provisional 2 × 5–8 target as a validated performance prescription until the athlete has tested the movement.**

The dip station's stability and intended loading must be verified before the first session.

## 4. Proposed Full Body B — Hinge Emphasis

**Focus:** Posterior chain, chest, back, rear deltoids and abdominal musculature.

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Decision |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Barbell Romanian Deadlift | 4 | Retain |
| 2 | Dumbbell Bench Press | 4 | Retain |
| 3 | Single-Arm Dumbbell Row | 3 | Reduce volume |
| 4 | Rear Delt Fly | 3 | Reduce volume |
| 5 | Bicycle Crunch | 3 | Move from C |

**Total: 5 movements, 17 working sets.**

### Changes

Remove Barbell Curl.

The athlete reports that this movement is uncomfortable for the shoulder. There is no compelling reason to retain this specific exercise when alternatives exist.

Do not replace Barbell Curl with another isolated biceps curl automatically.

The program already includes elbow-flexor stimulation through rowing movements and Hammer Curl in Session C.

Remove Russian Twist.

Move Bicycle Crunch from Session C into Session B, providing a more direct dynamic abdominal exercise.

### Bicycle Crunch coaching

Emphasize:

- Controlled trunk flexion and rotation.
- Avoid pulling the head or neck forward.
- Do not rush through alternating repetitions.
- Maintain consistent movement quality across sets.

Dead Bug should NOT replace Bicycle Crunch here.

Dead Bug already belongs to the athlete's separate Morning Posture Reset and serves a different, primarily motor-control purpose.

The strength program requires a meaningful abdominal training stimulus.

## 5. Proposed Full Body C — Lower Body & Shoulder Emphasis

**Focus:** Unilateral lower-body training, shoulder pressing, back, chest, elbow flexors and abdominal musculature.

| Order | Exercise | Working sets | Decision |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Dumbbell Reverse Lunge | 4 | Retain; move to first |
| 2 | Barbell Overhead Press | 3 | Reduce volume |
| 3 | Inverted Row | 3 | New |
| 4 | Incline Push-Up | 3 | Retain; reduce volume |
| 5 | Hammer Curl | 2 | Reduce volume |
| 6 | Supported Knee Raise | 3 | New |

**Total: 6 movements, 18 working sets.**

### Changes

Remove Barbell Hip Thrust entirely.

This removal must include its associated barbell-specific warm-up ramps.

Keep the existing general warm-up concept, but redesign the exercise-specific warm-up to prepare the athlete for Reverse Lunge and Overhead Press.

Do not replace Barbell Hip Thrust automatically with another loaded bridge variation.

The remaining weekly lower-body training includes:

- Goblet Squat
- Romanian Deadlift
- Reverse Lunge

Together these provide meaningful knee-extension, hip-extension, posterior-chain and unilateral lower-body stimulus.

Monitor results before determining whether additional direct glute volume is necessary.

Remove Dumbbell Squeeze Press.

Retain Incline Push-Up as the primary chest accessory in C.

This prevents excessive pressing redundancy while the athlete begins learning Parallel Bar Dips in A.

### Inverted Row

Introduce Inverted Row using the new parallel bars.

Primary objectives:

- Horizontal pulling strength.
- Back development.
- Additional elbow-flexor stimulus.
- Balanced pushing and pulling exposure across A/B/C.

Suggested initial prescription: 3 × 8–12 controlled reps, subject to movement testing.

Difficulty may be adjusted through body angle and foot placement, provided the station remains stable.

Do not begin with an aggressively horizontal body position if the athlete cannot control the movement.

### Supported Knee Raise

Introduce a supported parallel-bar knee raise as C's dedicated abdominal exercise.

Suggested initial prescription: 3 × 8–12.

The movement should include controlled posterior pelvic rotation where comfortable, rather than being performed solely as a hip-flexor-driven leg lift.

Technical requirements:

- Stable upper-body support.
- No swinging or momentum.
- Comfortable shoulder position.
- Controlled pelvic movement.
- Slow, controlled descent.

If shoulder or arm fatigue prevents meaningful abdominal work, retain a floor-based abdominal alternative instead.

The parallel-bar apparatus must not become a reason to compromise abdominal training quality.

## 8. Prescription model: pyramid versus straight sets

Do not automatically preserve four ascending pyramid rungs for every exercise.

The previous program used ascending load pyramids extensively.

For the optimized program, my preferred direction is:

**Primary compound lifts:** preserve their existing ascending pyramid prescriptions for the first revision, unless a specific exercise has a compelling reason to change.

**Accessories and newly introduced bodyweight movements:** use simpler straight-set prescriptions where appropriate.

Provisional structure:

- Major loaded compound exercises: typically 3–4 working sets.
- Secondary compound/accessory exercises: typically 2–3 working sets.
- Abdominal exercises: typically 2–3 working sets.

Do not extrapolate new loads or derive new pyramid rungs.

Keep current verified load prescriptions where the movement and set structure are unchanged.

Where a four-rung pyramid becomes three sets, the exact retained load/rep levels require explicit coach review.

Do not silently remove the first or last rung.

For exercise-specific carry-forward, use the athlete's actual completed logs as governed by the existing mechanism.

Do not reset all exercises to historical seed values merely because the program is being optimized.

## 9. Fit223 progression compatibility

The revised program must remain compatible with the existing log-based carry-forward progression system.

Preserve the principle:

**Completed exercise performance → next exercise prescription.**

Do not introduce a weekly AI review engine.

Do not introduce an arithmetic automatic overload algorithm.

Do not reinstate the retired automatic tempo ladder.

For continuing exercises:

- Preserve valid progression history where the exercise and prescription lineage remain compatible.
- Do not allow the prior four-set history to silently recreate a removed fourth working set.
- Treat the coach-authored structural reduction as authoritative.
- Avoid treating a prescribed set reduction as an athlete under-performance event.
- Preserve historical logs without rewriting them.

For new exercises:

- Begin with an explicit starting prescription.
- Use completed exercise logs for subsequent carry-forward.
- Do not infer initial capability from unrelated exercises.

Existing technique gates remain in effect for Dumbbell Lateral Raise and Rear Delt Fly.

The implementation must distinguish between deliberate program-authoring changes and athlete-generated log progression.

**Important:** The existing carry-forward rules were written around the previous prescription structure. The Agent Team must verify how a new program revision establishes its initial prescription state without old logs silently reversing the coach's changes.

## 11. Parallel-bar safety and exercise onboarding

The athlete owns a Decathlon Domyos adjustable freestanding parallel-bar station.

Before activating new exercises:

1. Verify the exact equipment model and its manufacturer's usage limits.
2. Confirm the station is stable and correctly assembled.
3. Confirm adequate clearance and nonslip footing.
4. Validate shoulder comfort and movement control.
5. Establish initial performance capability without a maximal-effort test.

Parallel Bar Dip and Inverted Row should not be assigned arbitrary external loads.

Supported Knee Raise must also be assessed for stability and effective abdominal engagement.

A successful equipment purchase does not automatically establish the athlete's readiness for advanced calisthenics movements.

If an exercise cannot be performed safely, the program must have a valid temporary substitution rather than encouraging the athlete to force the movement.

## 13. Evaluation period

Treat the first **3–4 completed A/B/C rotations** as an adaptation and evaluation period.

Track:

- Strength performance on primary movements.
- Completion of planned working sets.
- Rep quality and consistency.
- Total session duration.
- Fatigue in the final third of the session.
- Shoulder comfort during Dip and Inverted Row.
- Lower-body recovery after Squat, RDL and Reverse Lunge.
- Whether the supported knee raise actually provides adequate abdominal stimulus.

Do not interpret the first adaptation session as a test of maximum strength.

Do not respond to improved recovery by immediately increasing every exercise back to four sets.

The goal is sustainable progressive overload with better stimulus-to-fatigue efficiency.

# Fit223PCT — Coach Arbitration
## Mesocycle 2 Home Full Body Optimization
**Status: Coaching decisions for Agent Team validation and implementation**

This ruling resolves the nine outstanding questions from the Agent Team review.

The objective remains:

- Three Full Body sessions per week, rotating A → B → C.
- Fat loss with balanced hypertrophy and measurable strength progression.
- Reduced training fatigue and session duration.
- Introduction of the new parallel-bar equipment.
- Removal of Barbell Hip Thrust.
- Preservation of the existing log-based carry-forward model.

This revision is a deliberate structural change to the training program, not an athlete-generated progression event.

## Q2 — Inherit or restate?

**Decision: INHERIT for all continuing exercises and retained rungs.**

This applies to:

- Dumbbell Lateral Raise
- Plank
- Single-Arm Dumbbell Row
- Rear Delt Fly
- Bicycle Crunch
- Barbell Overhead Press
- Hammer Curl
- Incline Push-Up

The authored loads and rep targets in Q1 define the revised default prescription.

Where valid completed training history exists, use the athlete's actual logged performance from the corresponding retained old rung.

Do not reset strength progression merely because a structural program revision occurred.

For unchanged exercises, preserve compatible progression history.

For newly introduced exercises, begin with their explicitly authored starting prescriptions.

The deliberate new set count is authoritative and must not be reconstructed from historical four-set sessions.

## Q4 — Previously added extra working sets

**Decision: DO NOT inherit additional sets across this program revision boundary.**

This is an explicit refinement of the previous rule that completed athlete-added sets carry forward.

Within an unchanged prescription structure:

Completed added set → eligible for carry-forward.

Across a deliberate coach-authored structural revision:

The new coach-authored set count governs.

Example:

Old prescription: four working sets.

Athlete previously logged five working sets.

New prescription: three working sets.

Next session: THREE working sets.

The historical fifth set remains in the training log but does not automatically become part of the revised program.

Once the new revision is active, newly added and completed sets can again follow the established carry-forward rule.

Historical truth must be preserved without allowing it to override the deliberate volume reduction.

## Q5 — New parallel-bar movements

Three new movements are proposed.

Starting prescriptions are provisional technique-entry prescriptions, not claims of demonstrated athlete capacity.

### A — Parallel Bar Dip

**Starting prescription:**

- Sets: 2
- Reps: 5–8
- Load: Bodyweight, with reliable assistance if required
- Rest: 120 seconds
- Temporary substitute: Incline Push-Up

**Technique guidance:**

- Establish a stable supported position before descending.
- Keep the descent controlled.
- Use only a comfortable shoulder range of motion.
- Avoid excessively deep shoulder extension.
- Do not use momentum.
- Do not train to failure during initial adaptation.
- Stop if shoulder, sternum or anterior chest pain occurs.

Target approximately 2–3 technically clean repetitions remaining.

The athlete has not yet demonstrated an unassisted Dip baseline.

Therefore, if even assisted 5–8 reps cannot be performed safely, use the temporary substitute instead.

Do not require the athlete to complete a prescribed rep target at the expense of technique.

If the app cannot accurately represent assistance, do not falsely record assisted repetitions as equivalent to unassisted bodyweight performance.

Validate the exercise's load and assistance representation before rollout.

### C — Inverted Row

**Starting prescription:**

- Sets: 3
- Reps: 8–12
- Load: Bodyweight, difficulty adjusted by body angle
- Rest: 90 seconds
- Temporary substitute: Single-Arm Dumbbell Row

**Technique guidance:**

- Establish a stable body position.
- Keep the trunk controlled throughout.
- Pull the torso toward the bar without jerking.
- Avoid excessive lumbar extension.
- Control the lowering phase.
- Adjust body angle to maintain clean repetitions.

The starting body angle must be established through actual practice.

Changing body angle changes exercise difficulty even when external load remains unchanged.

Do not interpret all bodyweight Inverted Row performances as mechanically equivalent.

### C — Supported Knee Raise

**Starting prescription:**

- Sets: 3
- Reps: 8–12
- Load: Bodyweight
- Rest: 75 seconds
- Temporary substitute: Bicycle Crunch

**Technique guidance:**

- Maintain stable parallel-bar support.
- Avoid swinging.
- Raise the knees under control.
- Introduce comfortable posterior pelvic rotation near the top.
- Lower slowly and maintain trunk control.
- Do not force spinal flexion to obtain additional range.

The abdominal musculature should be meaningfully challenged.

If shoulder fatigue, grip or support instability becomes the primary limiting factor, use the temporary floor-based alternative.

### Substitution implementation

The substitute names above are coaching selections.

Before transcription, verify that their Exercise Library identities and substitution relationships are supported by the current schema.

Do not invent duplicate Exercise Library entries.

Do not create equipment-specific UI complexity solely to support this revision.

However, assisted and unassisted Dip performance must not be conflated in a way that produces misleading training history.

Retain:

2. Bodyweight Squat — 8 reps

## Q7 — Easier-day carry-forward

**Decision: The next normal session inherits from the last valid NORMAL session, not the authored baseline.**

An easier readiness day is a temporary workload modification.

It is not a permanent change to the athlete's demonstrated training capacity.

Example:

Normal session:

14 / 16 / 18 / 20 kg

Easier day:

14 / 16 / 18 kg

Next normal day:

Restore the normal four-rung prescription, including the last valid carried-forward value for the fourth rung.

Do not interpret omission of the top rung during an easier day as:

- athlete-requested permanent set deletion;
- failed progression;
- a new lower-volume baseline;
- a reason to reset the exercise to its original seed values.

The same principle applies to intentionally reduced loads or repetitions generated by the easier-day mechanism.

Easier-day logs remain part of historical truth but do not automatically redefine the next normal-day prescription.

Important distinction:

**Athlete deliberately changes a normal workout**  
→ existing carry-forward rules apply.

**App modifies a workout because of easier-day readiness**  
→ temporary prescription; resume the latest valid normal prescription next time.

An easier-day session must not revive a rung that was deliberately removed by the new program revision.

The latest coach-authored program structure still governs.

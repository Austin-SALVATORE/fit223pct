### B6. Substitute performance and carry-forward

**Decision: CONFIRMED for v1.**

A substitute exercise's completed performance does not modify the original exercise's prescription.

Example:

Parallel Bar Dip is substituted with Incline Push-Up.

The athlete's Push-Up performance must not be interpreted as Dip performance.

When the original Dip returns, it resumes from its last valid original-exercise prescription, or its coach-authored starting prescription if none exists.

Also do not use an incidental substitution log to rewrite the substitute exercise's independent progression chain.

This prevents cross-exercise progression contamination.

### B7. Exercise-specific preparation when swapped

**Decision: CONFIRMED.**

When a prescribed exercise is replaced by a substitute, hide preparation that belongs specifically to the original exercise.

For example:

If Dip is swapped out, hide the Dip-specific shoulder circles, Scapular Push-Up and Supported Dip Hold sequence.

Keep the normal session-opening warm-up.

Do not automatically create new substitute-specific preparation unless it has been explicitly authored.

### B8. Easier-day mechanics

**Decision: CONFIRMED, with a revision-boundary constraint.**

1. Preparation or movement content appearing only on easier days starts from its authored values.
2. When an easier-day rule removes a working set, remove the final set/rung, including an athlete-added final set where applicable.

However:

- Do not remove warm-up sets as though they were working sets.
- Do not allow an easier-day modification to redefine the normal-day prescription.
- Do not restore rungs deliberately removed by the revised program.
- Do not let an older program revision influence the set count of the new active revision.

The new program structure remains authoritative.

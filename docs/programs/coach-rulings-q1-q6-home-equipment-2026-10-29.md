## 2. Q2 — Barbell Bench Press and the Domyos TS 100

**Decision: Record the athlete's real-world experience, but distinguish exercise availability from approval of an improvised rack.**

The athlete has personally tested a Barbell Bench Press setup using the freestanding Domyos TS 100 bars as supports.

The athlete reports that the setup felt stable and usable during normal repetitions.

This feedback should be retained as actual equipment-use experience.

However, it does not establish manufacturer-approved barbell-rack capacity, safe reracking under uneven loading, or failure protection.

### Immediate product decision

Do not prescribe or endorse the TS 100 as a certified barbell rack or Smith machine.

For the current rackless home program, accept the Team's recommendation to remove Barbell Bench Press from routine substitution recommendations that assume this equipment can serve as a rack.

**Do not remove Barbell Bench Press from the general Exercise Library.**

The app must still be capable of truthfully recording Barbell Bench Press when performed with appropriate equipment.

If a user selects an exercise using unsupported equipment, logging accuracy and equipment suitability should be treated as separate concerns.

### Follow-up research

Evaluate whether any manufacturer-supported or purpose-built solution can make a barbell bench-press arrangement appropriate for the home environment.

The answer must address loading, unracking, reracking, stability, independent support movement and failure management.

Do not require the athlete to purchase new equipment merely to complete this research.

## 3. Q3 — More creative uses of the Dip Station

**Decision: YES — initiate a broader multi-equipment exercise feasibility review.**

This is an important clarification of the athlete's original request.

When the athlete introduced the Domyos TS 100, the intention was not limited to adding:

- Parallel Bar Dip
- Inverted Row
- Supported Knee Raise

The athlete wants Fit223 to explore how existing home equipment can be combined to expand practical training possibilities.

### Equipment available

- Domyos TS 100 independent adjustable parallel bars
- 7.75 kg barbell and weight plates
- Adjustable dumbbells
- Adjustable training bench
- Indoor bike

### Research direction

Explore combinations involving:

**A. Barbell + Parallel Bars**

Identify mechanically feasible movement concepts, including those involving supporting, positioning or integrating the barbell with the parallel bars.

The athlete has specifically expressed interest in concepts resembling barbell bench-press support and rack-assisted squat variations.

Investigate these concepts rather than dismissing the entire category without analysis.

However, distinguish:

- Physically possible setups
- Manufacturer-supported uses
- Configurations with verified stability and load capacity
- Configurations requiring purpose-built safety equipment
- Configurations that should not be used

The TS 100 is not a guided Smith machine. Do not label a free-bar squat setup as a Smith squat.

Do not recommend unsupported loaded barbell configurations as ready-to-use training movements.

**B. Parallel Bars + Adjustable Bench**

Explore combinations that safely change leverage, elevation, body position or support during pressing, pulling, lower-body and core exercises.

**C. Parallel Bars + Dumbbells**

Explore whether the apparatus can provide useful balance or positioning support for dumbbell-based exercises without introducing unsafe loading on the bars.

**D. Additional Parallel-Bar Calisthenics**

Evaluate:

- Supported L-Sit progressions
- Leg Raise progressions
- Parallel-bar Push-Up variations
- Assisted unilateral lower-body movements
- Additional supported core movements

Do not assume every candidate belongs in the current program.

### Expected deliverable

Produce a short **Home Equipment Combination Feasibility Matrix**.

For each candidate, include:

1. Exercise name and intended muscle groups.
2. Equipment combination.
3. Training benefit compared with existing movements.
4. Feasibility and safety classification.
5. Relevant manufacturer limitations.
6. Whether it could replace an existing A/B/C exercise.
7. Any necessary new Library entry, illustration or equipment metadata.

Classify candidates as:

- Suitable with existing verified equipment use
- Conditional — requires further validation
- Requires purpose-built equipment
- Not recommended

**The goal is creative but evidence-based equipment integration, not an automatic increase in exercise count.**

No additional movement is approved for the live program merely by appearing in the feasibility review.

## 4. Q4 — Cross-Implement Substitution Starting Weight

**Decision: Option (b).**

Use the substitute exercise's most recent valid logged weight if available.

Otherwise, provide no suggested weight and let the athlete explicitly select one.

Do not copy load values between incompatible implements.

Examples:

Dumbbell Bench Press → Barbell Bench Press

Do not transfer a per-dumbbell prescription into a total-barbell prescription.

Single-Arm Dumbbell Row → Inverted Row

Do not display dumbbell weights for a bodyweight movement.

Requirements:

- Preserve correct load units.
- Preserve implement-specific adjustment increments.
- Use only valid performance history for suggestions.
- Do not reuse corrupted or incorrectly identified historical sets.
- Do not invent equivalent loads for bodyweight movements.

Keep the substitution workflow simple and consistent with the existing workout interface.

## 5. Q5 — Weight Logged Above Equipment Ceiling

**Decision: Option O1 — Carry forward the athlete's actual logged weight without clamping.**

The equipment ceiling must not act as a hard limit on manual logging.

Example:

Program ceiling: 20 kg  
Actual logged load: 22 kg  
Next valid prescription: 22 kg

No additional automatic increase is applied.

This confirms the existing direct-log carry-forward model.

### Approve the weight selector fix immediately

- Remove the hard maximum from manual weight input.
- Do not silently clamp recorded loads.
- Preserve the exact user-entered load.
- Keep equipment ceilings informational.
- Display a non-blocking warning when appropriate.
- Do not show the original exercise's equipment ceiling after swapping to a different implement.
- Do not automatically update equipment inventory from above-ceiling logs.

Technique-gated Dumbbell Lateral Raise and Rear Delt Fly retain their specific load rules.

### Historical bench-press record

The 24 October Barbell Bench Press was incorrectly capped at 20 kg total.

The athlete will supply the actual lifted loads.

Do not invent the missing values or treat the stored 20 kg as verified performance.

Preserve the original record and clearly distinguish known data corruption from legitimate completed-load history.

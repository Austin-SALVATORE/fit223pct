---

This specification defines the first guided stretching routine for
Fit223.

The purpose is **recovery**, not flexibility training or yoga. Every
stretch is selected to reduce accumulated tightness from the Phase 1
strength program while remaining simple enough for every user.

## Design principles

### Objective

Improve recovery and prepare the body for the next training session.

The routine is **not** intended to:

- Maximize flexibility
- Improve split or mobility range
- Become a yoga session
- Induce fatigue

### Style

- Static stretching only
- Timed holds only
- No repetitions
- No sets
- No progression
- Hands-free playback
- Auto-advance after every hold

### Equipment

- Exercise mat / floor
- Wall

No other equipment. (The original v1 also declared an adjustable bench;
the coach removed it on 28 Jul — see the addendum — because no stretch
in the routine used it.)

### Movement principles

- Minimize transitions between standing and floor positions.
- Complete both sides before moving to the next stretch.
- Use only stretches that provide clear recovery value.
- Every stretch must justify two illustrations.

## Guided routine

| # | Stretch | Hold | Per side | Cue |
|---|---------|------|----------|-----|
| 1 | Wall Chest Stretch | 30 s | Yes | Keep your shoulder relaxed and feel the stretch across the front of your chest. |
| 2 | Wall Calf Stretch | 30 s | Yes | Keep your back heel firmly on the floor. |
| 3 | Standing Hamstring Stretch | 30 s | Yes | Hinge from the hips and keep your back long. |
| 4 | Standing Quadriceps Stretch | 30 s | Yes | Keep your knees together and gently push your hips forward. |
| 5 | Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch | 30 s | Yes | Tuck your pelvis slightly before shifting forward. |
| 6 | Figure-4 Glute Stretch | 30 s | Yes | Keep your lower back relaxed throughout the hold. |
| 7 | Seated Butterfly Stretch | 30 s | No | Sit tall and let your knees fall naturally. |
| 8 | Child's Pose | 45 s | No | Relax your shoulders and breathe deeply into your back. |

### Playback order

1. Wall Chest Stretch
2. Wall Calf Stretch
3. Standing Hamstring Stretch
4. Standing Quadriceps Stretch
5. Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch
6. Figure-4 Glute Stretch
7. Seated Butterfly Stretch
8. Child's Pose

The sequence intentionally progresses from standing to kneeling to
floor positions.

## Coach rulings

### A. Lead-in duration

**8 seconds (fixed)** before every stretch.

### B. Side order

Always perform **left → right**.

### C. Target total duration

- Hold time: **435 s (7 min 15 s)**
- Lead-ins: **64 s**
- Total routine: **~8 min 20 s**

## Recovery card

Replace:

> Stretch · 10–15 min

With:

> Guided Stretch · 8–10 min

## Addendum — coach decisions, 28 Jul

Three ambiguities surfaced by structural validation of v1. Each one
changes an illustration, none changes a hold, an order or a cue.

1. **Adjustable bench removed** from the equipment list. No stretch in
   the routine benefited from it enough to justify declaring it, and
   unused declared equipment is only complexity.
2. **Figure-4 Glute Stretch is supine**, lying on the floor — not
   seated. Easier for beginners, more stable, better glute isolation,
   and it follows naturally from the Half-Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch
   as the routine transitions to the floor. Entry frame is the supine
   Figure-4 setup; held frame is the stretch.
3. **Both standing stretches use the wall for balance.** Standing
   Hamstring: one hand lightly against the wall. Standing Quadriceps:
   one hand against the wall, the other holding the ankle. Recovery day
   is not a balance assessment — attention belongs on the stretch. The
   wall is support only and is never the source of the stretch.

**Art direction that follows from (3):** every standing stretch is
illustrated with the same simple wall reference, so the visual language
is consistent and no user has to wonder whether wall support is
optional for a particular stretch. Four of the eight stretches — Wall
Chest, Wall Calf, Standing Hamstring, Standing Quadriceps — therefore
carry a wall in frame.

Note that the cues are unchanged, so the wall is communicated by the
illustration alone. That is deliberate: the cue is the only prose on
screen during a hold and is reserved for what to feel or avoid.
